# Find LinkedIn Conversation Opportunities

Identify live LinkedIn conversations where the user's offering is relevant because the poster has expressed a concrete need, question, launch, customer problem, or operating goal. Optimize for genuine contribution and qualified learning rather than comment volume.

## Establish the offering

Understand the product or service, target customer, strongest outcomes, proof, limitations, and desired next step. Ask only when a missing fact would materially change the search or response. Do not invent capabilities, results, customer relationships, or compatibility.

## Search for expressed needs

Use current LinkedIn post search when access is available. Search for language that reveals an active situation rather than merely matching the product category, including:

- launches, redesigned websites, new services, and current booking;
- recurring customer questions, objections, confusion, or education needs;
- lead-generation, qualification, conversion, support, or discoverability problems;
- posts describing a workflow the offering could extend;
- complementary consultants or agencies who may be partners rather than customers.

Vary searches deliberately and stop when additional results are weak or repetitive. Do not scrape broadly, automate engagement, or treat every topical mention as a prospect.

## Qualify each post

Prefer posts where:

1. the poster states a concrete business situation or customer need;
2. the offering provides a specific, explainable benefit;
3. the poster appears able to evaluate, buy, recommend, or implement it;
4. a public response can contribute without hijacking the discussion; and
5. the proposed claim is supported by the post or verified public evidence.

Classify each result as **direct prospect**, **potential partner**, **conversation only**, or **not suitable**. Separate strong results from weak matches instead of filling a requested count.

## Capture durable evidence

For each recommended result, record:

- poster and organization when visible;
- a short identifying summary of the post;
- why the offering is relevant;
- relationship type and confidence;
- the actual direct post permalink.

When LinkedIn search cards do not expose a permalink, use the post menu's **Copy link to post** action and verify the resulting URL. Never substitute a profile page or search-results page while describing it as a direct post link. If no direct link can be verified, label it unavailable.

## Draft useful-first public replies

Write a distinct comment for each post. The comment should:

- respond to the poster's actual point;
- add an observation, implication, or thoughtful question;
- sound natural for the user's voice;
- avoid a link, generic compliment, immediate demo offer, or canned product pitch;
- mention the offering only when the post explicitly invites that comparison or the mention is genuinely additive.

For most direct prospects, use a two-stage path:

1. contribute publicly without pitching;
2. mention the offering only after the poster engages or explicitly asks.

For peer or partner posts, a light disclosure such as “I’m working on a related approach” may be appropriate when it advances the discussion.

Do not reuse substantially identical comments across posts. Do not promise leads, rankings, conversions, or other outcomes without evidence.

## Preserve user control

Research, analyze, rank, and draft without separate authorization. Do not post, comment, react, follow, connect, message, submit a form, or otherwise represent the user without explicit authorization immediately before that action. Show the exact recipient, post, and final wording before any authorized communication.

Never use unauthorized automation to create LinkedIn engagement. Follow LinkedIn's current professional-community and anti-spam rules; verify them when the user asks for policy or risk guidance.

## Output

Return a compact ranked list or table containing the direct post link, identifying post summary, relationship type, specific benefit, and copy-ready public reply. State which opportunities deserve attention first and why. Distinguish identified posts from contacted people and actual prospects.

Completion requires verified direct links for recommended posts or an explicit unavailable label, evidence-based fit analysis, distinct useful-first replies, and confirmation that no engagement was sent unless separately authorized.
