# Produce Visual Timelapse

Turn a transformation into a persistent world whose state changes along an explicit timeline. Keep geography coherent while the camera moves. Treat this as a production workflow, not an automatic guarantee of a photorealistic or historically exact film.

## Establish scope and mode

Select Research, Blueprint, Prototype, Produce, Adapt, or Review. Combine only modes authorized by the request. Reuse compatible approved research, scene files, assets, timelines, and renders; invoke only missing or stale stages.

Record subject, geographic extent, time range, factual versus fictional intent, milestones, target duration, aspect ratio, visual style, camera approach, audio, exact labels, output destination, available tools, and budget. Infer reversible creative choices. Ask only for missing decisions that materially change scope or cost. Default a first proof to 20–30 seconds and about five milestones when the user has not requested a larger film.

Read [references/reference-style.md](references/reference-style.md) when adopting the supplied aerial historical reference. Analyze accessible footage or use available video-analysis tools; do not claim direct viewing from a transcript or automated description. Keep style evidence separate from historical evidence.

## Research changes

Build an evidence ledger with stable milestone IDs, date precision, location, event type, source URL or supplied provenance, confidence, and uncertainty. Verify material historical claims from authoritative records where accessible. Distinguish construction start, opening, expansion, demolition, and proposed completion. Never show a planned development as completed history. Use approximate or ranged dates honestly; do not fabricate a precise year from uncertain evidence.

For fiction, use creator-approved canon and mark proposed changes. For illustrative transformations, label the sequence illustrative in the production package. A source video is style inspiration, not historical proof. Do not copy its soundtrack or extract reusable assets without appropriate rights.

## Build the world and time contracts

Read [references/world-and-timeline.md](references/world-and-timeline.md) before implementation. Maintain stable entity IDs, coordinate origin, units, map projection when relevant, terrain, orientation, scale, lifecycle intervals, asset revisions, replacement relationships, and source references. Separate confirmed geometry from simplified or inferred geometry.

Define a piecewise mapping between historical time and video time. Pause on major events, accelerate quiet periods, and reserve a final reveal. Use the same mapping for entity state and the year counter. Specify whether construction is represented schematically or modeled as stages. Do not let two incompatible replacements occupy the same site simultaneously.

Plan camera keyframes in world coordinates, targets, lenses, easing, cuts, and safe areas. Preserve spatial anchors across moves. Avoid excessive spins, unexplained scale jumps, and camera collision. A fixed camera is an option, not a requirement.

## Select a feasible production path

- **Persistent 3D:** Prefer an editable scene in Blender or a comparable renderer when geography, lifecycle, or camera precision matters.
- **2.5D/map:** Use layered vector or map animation when budgets or available tools make detailed 3D impractical. Clearly identify the chosen representation.
- **Generative/hybrid:** Use AI for asset concepts, textures, or bounded transitions. Do not represent independently generated scenes as a geographically exact world. Reject continuity drift or obtain agreement to an illustrative treatment.

Discover actual tool capabilities before choosing a path. For Higgsfield 3D Jutsu, obtain a real project ID, inspect the current scene/revision, perform one coherent mutation at a time against the observed revision, settle operations, then inspect artifacts. A GLB preview or successful scene edit is not a rendered MP4. Verify animation export and rendering capability separately. Never invent tool arguments, assets, revisions, or provider support.

Optionally invoke the installed produce-reference-driven-video skill for live provider preflight, chargeable generations, reference roles, job tracking, audio, and deterministic finishing. Load its current instructions rather than copying them. It is not required for research or deterministic local rendering. If unavailable, perform only supported stages and identify the precise missing capability.

Before chargeable work, follow the provider's current approval rules and quote the full expected batch, including retries only when authorized. Do not publish, buy credits, create accounts, or send media externally without applicable authorization. Existing session authorization remains valid.

## Prove continuity before full production

Render a low-resolution proof containing the initial state, one addition, one replacement/removal if relevant, a camera move, a time overlay, and the final state. Inspect representative frames plus continuous playback where possible. Scale production only after geography, event order, readability, and render feasibility pass. Do not silently shorten an explicitly requested film; communicate material scope changes.

Keep a production manifest with scene/shot IDs, milestone IDs, video timing, world dates, camera data, asset versions, source evidence, prompts if used, job/operation IDs, costs, and output revisions. Save reproducible scene/project files and checkpoints to the authorized destination.

## Finish and review

Render exact dates and labels deterministically. Keep overlays independent of generated scenery. Synchronize effects with events; use licensed or authorized music and preserve audio provenance. Add narration or speech captions only when requested or already authorized.

Check event order and date precision; entity persistence and removal; terrain, scale, roads and locations; camera continuity; year-counter alignment; text spelling and safe areas; final-state completeness; audio timing and intelligibility; and output duration, dimensions, frame rate, streams, and accessibility.

Repair timeline, labels, camera keys, and state logic before regenerating media. Classify checks as passed, failed, blocked, manual, or not run. Automated scene analysis supplements visual review; it does not certify historical accuracy or pixel-level continuity.

## Deliver and stop honestly

Deliver the requested blueprint or the actual reviewed video, plus the reusable timeline/world manifest and editable project when requested. State approximations and unresolved consequential defects. Never present a storyboard, scene preview, generation job, or unrendered project as a finished video.

GitHub is unnecessary unless repository storage is requested. Before repository writes, follow the destination's instructions and the shared repository-readiness guide when available; confirm identity, destination, visibility, permissions, and branch policy. Keep private research and media out of public repositories unless explicitly authorized for that audience.

Completion means the requested mode's artifacts are accessible and verified; production additionally requires an actual playable final render. Missing tools block the affected production step, not independent research or planning.
