# Creative Development Lenses

Use a few targeted lenses rather than asking every question.

## Premise and theme

- What human tension does the speculative premise externalize?
- What promise should the audience feel repeatedly?
- Which apparent truth will the world complicate?
- What must remain strange rather than fully explained?

## Rules and consequences

- What are the limits, costs, access conditions, and failure modes?
- Who controls the rule, who exploits it, and who is excluded?
- What workaround would ordinary people invent?
- What institution emerges after decades or centuries?
- What unintended consequence becomes more important than the original invention?

## Culture and ordinary life

- What does a child learn before they understand why?
- What is polite, shameful, sacred, funny, or aspirational?
- What does a typical home, meal, workplace, celebration, or funeral reveal?
- Which groups interpret the same tradition differently?
- What does the culture debate internally?

## Power and legitimacy

- Why do people comply: trust, habit, benefit, fear, or lack of alternatives?
- What resources make power durable?
- Where does formal authority diverge from practical control?
- Which bargain stabilizes society, and what is breaking it?
- How do marginalized people exercise agency?

## History and memory

- Which past event still structures current choices?
- Who preserves the record and who cannot appear in it?
- What does everyone "know" that may be false?
- Which historical change occurred gradually rather than in one dramatic event?
- How do ruins, borders, language, or rituals carry memory?

## Character and story

- How does the world make the character's desire possible and dangerous?
- What belief helps the character survive but prevents growth?
- Which relationship crosses a social or institutional fault line?
- What ordinary decision can escalate into a systemic conflict?
- Which viewpoint reveals a different world without requiring a new setting?

## Distinctiveness

- If familiar genre nouns are removed, what remains unique?
- Which two elements interact in a way rarely explored?
- What obvious genre assumption can be inverted without becoming gimmicky?
- Which detail could only exist because of this world's causal history?
- What should remain simple so the distinctive element has room to breathe?

## Continuity stress tests

- Can travel, communication, and knowledge move as the plot assumes?
- Do ages, dates, distances, resources, and institutional capacities align?
- Would a powerful capability erase an important conflict? If not, why?
- Does an exception reveal character or theme, or merely rescue the plot?
- What else must change if this fact changes?
